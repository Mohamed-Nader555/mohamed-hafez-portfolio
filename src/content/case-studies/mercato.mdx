## The problem

Getting noticed by a football academy usually depends on who happens to be watching a particular match, not on how a player actually performs on the ball. A written trial request or a résumé line can't show a first touch or a finish the way thirty seconds of footage can. A client wanted a way for players outside the traditional academy pipeline to put their skills in front of the people who scout for clubs.

The gap isn't a lack of talented players; it's a lack of a channel between them and the agents who could scout them. A club agent can't attend every local match in every region, and a player without an academy connection has no obvious way to reach one. Short-form video closes that distance without either side needing to be in the same city.

## Who it was for

Players trying to get scouted outside the academy system, and the club agents looking for talent they wouldn't otherwise see. For a player, the app is a way to put a first touch or a finish in front of someone who can act on it. For an agent, it's a way to browse verified footage instead of relying on word of mouth or a scouting trip that may not turn anything up.

## My role

I customised and extended a short-video app foundation into a football-talent product. That meant adapting the recording and feed experience to a sports-scouting purpose, adding the verification, subscription, and notification layers a two-sided platform needs, and delivering the result as a working Android application for the client. Most of the product decisions were about what to add on top of that foundation rather than what to build from a blank screen: verification, subscription tiers, and the notification loop between a clip and the agent watching it.

## What I built

The core loop is short and deliberate: record a clip, trim it to the useful part, apply a filter if it helps, and upload it to a feed an agent can browse. Verification badges give agents a way to trust a profile before they spend time watching its clips, and the subscription plans give players a reason to keep a profile current rather than uploading once and disappearing. None of this works as one-sided software; the notification layer exists specifically so an agent's interest reaches a player quickly enough to matter.

## How it works

Clips move from capture through editing and upload into a feed that other accounts play back, with accounts, subscriptions, and push notifications feeding into that same feed.

## Outcome

Mercato Star Finder was delivered to the client as a working Android application; it was not published to a public store listing.

## What I learned

> Two-sided platforms need real value for both sides, not just the side that's easiest to build for.

It's tempting to focus on the player-facing recording experience, because that's the flashier half of the product. But a football-talent platform only works if the agent side is just as considered: verification, subscriptions, and notifications aren't features bolted onto the recording tool; they're the reason an agent trusts what they're watching enough to act on it.
