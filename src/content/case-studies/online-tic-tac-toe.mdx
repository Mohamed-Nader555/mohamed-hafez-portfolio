## The problem

Tic-tac-toe is trivial to play on one screen and surprisingly fiddly to get right over a network: both players' boards have to agree after every move, invitations and disconnects need handling, and an offline mode still needs an opponent worth playing against.

## What I built

I built it end to end: a JavaFX client and server that support offline play against a friend or a Minimax AI with adjustable difficulty, and online play with a user list, invitations, authentication, and scores. Finished games are recorded move by move so they can be replayed afterward.

<FeatureGrid items={frontmatter.features} />

The server has to be the source of truth once a game is online: a move only counts once the server accepts it and pushes the resulting board back to both clients, which is what keeps the two sides from disagreeing about the state of the board.

## What I learned

> Even a simple game becomes a real distributed-systems problem the moment two players stop sharing a screen.

Building the online mode taught me more about client-server state agreement than the game itself was complicated enough to need, which is exactly why it was worth building.
