## The problem

Planned lessons cover planned situations. Real shifts don't: a nurse might need to explain a delay to a family, or ask a colleague to repeat an order, and want the right English for it now. The founder's PRD asked for an always-available coach for exactly these moments, with an MVP due in three days.

## Who it was for

Nurses and clinicians preparing for, or already working in, English-speaking hospitals. They need one clear, safe answer to the situation in front of them, then a quick way to practise it. Eklan is training software, so the coach helps with communication and stays out of clinical decisions.

## My role

I turned the founder's PRD into a buildable design in one day and built the coach in three: the structured-output layer, the output checker, English and Korean PII redaction, the eval suite, the drill job queue, audio and pronunciation practice, and the interface. Admins can change who has access (plan, pilot list and a kill switch) without a redeploy.

## How it works

A question arrives as text or a voice note and is redacted before anything is stored. The coach answers as structured output, rules in code check the answer, and the chat is locked once the problem is solved. A background job then turns the solved chat into a personal drill.

## What I learned

> Three days is enough if the first one is spent deciding what not to build.

The day spent agreeing the design with the founder did more for the deadline than any shortcut in the code. Gating the release on an eval run, rather than on a demo that looked right, is what made it safe to ship that fast.

## What I'd do next

Grow the eval suite from the questions learners actually ask once the coach is in wider use, keeping the redaction rules in front of anything that gets saved.
