## The problem

Eklan's live classes are where learners speak the most, and where tutors hear the most errors. But a tutor can't write down every slip for every learner while running a lesson, so most of that evidence was lost when the call ended. The transcript holds it all, buried in an hour of conversation between several people.

## Who it was for

Tutors who want each learner's next practice set to come from what that learner actually got wrong, and learners who get drills built around their own sentences instead of generic exercises.

## My role

I built the extraction pipeline end to end: the LLM tool call with redaction and a length cap, the deterministic dedupe and sensitive-topic filter, the tutor editing workflow with conflict-safe saves, and the link into drill generation.

## How it works

A tutor links a transcript to a learner's week. The transcript is redacted and capped, one LLM tool call pulls out that learner's mistakes, and code dedupes and filters them. The tutor then edits the list and sends the chosen groups to drill generation.

## Results

I evaluated the pipeline on a real recorded lesson against the mistakes a tutor had found by hand. The best runs matched 7 of the tutor's 8, with no duplicates across groups after the dedupe fix. Each analysis took 17–39 s per class, measured in testing against the live models.

## What I learned

> Let the model find candidates. Let code and a tutor decide what counts.

The model was good at spotting mistakes in a messy, multi-speaker transcript. It was not the right place to enforce rules about privacy, duplication or tone. Splitting the work that way made the tool something tutors could trust with their learners.

## What I'd do next

Evaluate on more lessons and more tutors, so the 7 of 8 becomes a measured rate rather than a best run.
