## The problem

A ninja platformer only works if it stays smooth. The client needed a game that ran without lag or dropped frames on everyday Android phones, not just the newest devices, since a laggy jump kills a platformer in its first level.

## What I built

I built The Death Ninja in Unity: a side-scrolling platformer with multiple levels, coin and life counters, a boss fight, and a choice between two playable characters, Ninja Boy and Ninja Girl, each ending in their own win screen. The main menu leads into levels where the ninja hops across platforms and hazards, collecting coins and losing lives, before a final boss encounter. I optimised rendering and touch-input latency so movement stayed responsive on lower-end hardware, triaged crashes and ANRs reported after release, and handled Android App Bundle signing and a staged rollout through Google Play Console. The client published the finished game to Google Play under their own store listing; that listing is no longer available, because the client stopped maintaining it.

## Tech stack

The game is built entirely in Unity, targeting Android, with releases signed and rolled out through Google Play Console.

## What I learned

> Smooth input on cheap hardware matters more to players than any single feature.

Optimising for lower-end Android devices taught me that responsiveness is a feature in itself, not a polish pass layered on top of the game design after the fact.
